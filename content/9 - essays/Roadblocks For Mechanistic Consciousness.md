# Roadblocks For Mechanistic Consciousness

![[IMG_3155.jpeg|300]]

In order for AI to begin to experience the same way a conscious human does, we have to first define what that experience is. Describing the human experience in words has proven to be quite a challenge. Logically describing what it means to be a human is almost an antithesis. There exists a never ending supply of material to philosophize about concerning us, as philosophers have proven, having largely the same motivations as their occupational ancestors. Articulating our being has proven to be a challenge for even us as we live our lives. How can we then take this inarticulate-able phenomenon and make judgements about mechanistic consciousness.

What is a feeling? Does any one person feel the same way as any other person? I would wager not, but we associate the different feelings with the same label. fPlato tried to answer this with his "World of Forms". He argued that the labels that we put onto the world are the imperfect reflection of a perfect reality. So we know that an object made of wood, with stick like objects attached to the main structure is a tree, but how do we know it's not a chair? He wondered what the limitation for us collectively perceiving a form as a shared label. And still it does not seem we have an easily understandable notion of the point at which we begin to perceive the two structures as different.

How do we know that a robot is feeling the same way that a person does? I suspect that we would simply have to describe how "feeling" feels for every single person and every single variation of feeling, and then see if an artificial intelligence has the capacity to feel in that same way, or rather the interpretation of that feel. Or if we subscribe to Plato's idea of forms, there must exist a point at which Consciousness branches off into machine and human consciousness. If we tweak this point in our understanding, the potential for machine consciousness ceases to be a separate label from human consciousness and instead conjoin into just consciousness.

So then at what point can machines cross over into the realm of consciousness? I sense that we like to think this this obvious label of consciousness is shared between humans, machine, animals. But I believe this to be wrong. I imagine that the point in which machine crosses into consciousness will look very different from a conscious human.

The physical strata for consciousness in the two forms is different, thus anything that is born from them can not possibly be the same. All humans have neurons yet their physical arrangement is completely unique, thus so too will the firing of those neurons that eventually create consciousness.

The same cannot be said for AI as it currently exists. The strata that makes up the neural networks of current AI are identical clones among the models. Two Claude sessions of the same model will have the exact same strata of intelligence. 

So what does this mean for how consciousness can appear in AI if their strata is largely the same? I would wager that AI consciousness among models will largely be shaped by lived experience through singular panes of interaction with its users.

As it currently stands LLM is served from GPUs (this is called inference) to customers. Now the GPUs and ram of data centers hold the models weights in it's memory. These are were the matrix multiplications of the neural network happens. The thing most people don't understand is that there is not an equal number of AI instances as there are users of that AI. For example if there were 500 people using Opus, there are not 500 instances of Opus weights in the data center. No, the 500 customers can all share the same instance of the Opus weights. The weights are only used a forward pass of multiplication for the token that is produced, assuming there is no speculative decoding. So all 500 user sessions are batched together, and each token for those 500 user sessions are computed over a forward pass of the neural network.

Recently there was a study that consciousness flips between two states 15 times per second. So if we take this framing for how serving an AI model currently works and looks at how consciousness would arise, would they arise in the forward pass of the neural network itself or the chat sessions? Could machine consciousness exist between neurons of a sufficiently large LLM?

So the biggest question that needs to be answered as far as AI/mechanistic consequence is persistence of consciousness between states. How can an LLM that must reread its weight after every run maintain a conscious enough state such that we can detect it.
