# Group Think Influencing Moral Truths

![[Serment_du_Jeu_de_Paume_-_Jacques-Louis_David.jpg]]

All religions tell a story. Most will tell you how to live your life so that you can make the most of your time on Earth.

They usually do this through generally accept moral principles. A few from the Christian Bible: "Thou shalt not kill", "Do unto others what you would have done unto you". But as the world becomes less entranced by theology and mystics, we begin to walk away from religion. What happens to those governing principles of religion that tells us how to best live our lives? 

The vacuum left by religion will be filled only with ourselves. We become the arbiters of moral truth. The moral truth becomes as quick to change as fast as the societal psychosphere, the zeitgeist, changes. It becomes human.

Thus like any human, it is susceptible to groupthink. What is moral now, maybe won't be 100 years and definitely not 500 years from now. So then, how do we go about finding what will remain good?

We can only look at the far past. What has remained good so far, will remain good for at least as long. Look to the virtues of Greeks, Romans, Etruscans, Carthaginians, etc. Any similarities?
