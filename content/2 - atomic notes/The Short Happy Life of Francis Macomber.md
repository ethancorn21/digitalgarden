# The Short Happy Life of Francis Macomber

Francis Macomber, Margaret Macomber, Robert Wilson

Margaret and Francis have an extremely toxic relationship. Francis is too rich for Margaret to leave and Margaret is too pretty for Francis to leave. Maragret routinely cucks her husband.

Francis wounds a Lion while hunting, they chase it into the bush and when the lion charges Francis and Robert, Francis run while Robert stays put and fires. Francis is seen by Robert and Margaret as a coward, and later Margaret sneaks away to Robert's tent in the middle of the night. Robert despises her (in reality all American women), but the wive's of his patrons sneaking into his tent is nothing new. He views this as simply part of what they paid for. The wealthy husbands gets his trophy and the wives get theirs. So you can see how Robert Wilson's talk of honor and hyper-masculinity at fault because he exists to serve himself under the guise of serving others.

Wilson makes a fuss of never hunting from vehicles as it takes the sportsmanship out of the hunt and it is illegal. But, during the buffalo hunt, he gets fired up alongside Francis (who he cucked) and they both start shooting from the vehicle. 

Francis Macomber's awakening during the buffalo hunt. His wife realizes this and her control that she has over him is now gone (Wilson makes note of this). Knowing that her control over her husband is over and the toxic cycle of their marriage is now over, Francis shoots her husband in the head as the last buffalo is charging him.

Wilson immediately gains complete control over her by hinting that it looks like she did it on purpose, and this control is important because he wants to control the only living person who can testify that they were shooting from the vehicles (which is illegal and he would lose his business). 

Francis Macomber - no self respect, no risk tolerance.

Magaret Macomber - user. Cucks her husband to dominate him.

Robert Wilson - At first depicted by virtue of masculinity. As story goes on, realize he's a POS. Willing to compromise morals that he tells others he adheres to.
