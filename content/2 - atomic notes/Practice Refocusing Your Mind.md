# Practice Refocusing Your Mind

Practice refocusing your mind when thinking about a problem.

Already, when you audit your thought processes, you notice how often you catch yourself off track from where you originally wanted to go. A sidetrack creates a side track and eventually instead of depth of thought, you create breadth of thought.

This is not necessarily a bad thing, but already your day is spent constantly context switching between sources of information and superficial insights.

You need to drill deeper into thoughts. You can't suffice on minor reflection and then slip away onto a new thought without notice. You have to practice refocusing your minds eye back onto the problem at hand. It's a skill, you must practice it.

For example, when you practice mindful concentration. Set out a concrete topic. Ask yourself an inquiring question like, "Have I truly changed and grown in the last year?" This is the singular focus of the session. You have to get into the habit of regularly asking and remembering to check in with yourself and make sure you are still thinking about the question.

You will find yourself in a loop. One thought will lead to another which will lead back to the first thought. Break this loop by playing contrarian with the viewpoint you are currently leaning towards. For example, if I was leaning toward not having changed and grown much in the last year and I was stuck in a thought loop, I will recognize the loop and break out of it by asking, "How have I changed mentally, physically, emotionally ; what supports this?"
