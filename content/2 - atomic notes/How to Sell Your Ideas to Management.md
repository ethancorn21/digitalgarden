# How to Sell Your Ideas to Management

You must first start within yourself. Do you understand the idea that you are trying to sell completely? Do you understand all of the drawbacks and benefits? Are you over invested into your idea such that you are blinded to obvious conclusions?

Once you have truly thought over your idea, create a written report and a slide deck. If your idea is good, these will be forwarded higher up the chain, so make sure it looks, sounds, and reads good. No grammatical errors or stylistic mistakes. 

The content of both the report and the slide deck should include 1) an executive summary with little technical jargon. Include why the business should care. 2) Then you can lay on a multi-page technical write up. 3) include timelines, estimates, constraints, and drawbacks. 

The goal is to give your manager the reasons why you feel passionately about your decision, help them see why you think this is the right decision, so that they can then pass that information onto their superiors. 

Be passionate but not blinded and emotional. Always be polite, if you are told no then take it with grace. You may need to escalate if you are told no, but let the person who told you "no" that.

All managers, directors, presidents, execs want to know is: how much you need, how long you need, what your justification is. Appeal to their desire to make their boss happy and in doing so make themselves look good. So even if there boss is not in the room, appeal to what their boss would like.

Consider answering these questions in your reports and presentations:
- How will your idea save money?
	- CapEx ex: it will replace multiple pieces of equipment that need to be replaced anyways
	- OpEx ex: it will reduce personnel costs
	- ROI ex: the cost vs value gained = X over X years
- How will your idea increase revenues?
- How will your idea increase profits?
- How will your idea make the department and your boss / boss's boss look good?
