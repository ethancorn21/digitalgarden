# Confounding Others With Your Own Brilliance

>I have spent weeks designing elegant IP schemes for large companies. The scheme for one client was so impressive it could be described only as magnificent. It was a masterpiece. The scalability was measured in decades, and the flexibility was unheard of. It was so impressive that only the two of us who designed it understand it to this day.
> \- Gary A. Donahue, Network Warrior

The most elegant, beautiful, complex solution is not always the answer and in fact most time it will never be. You must conduct [[Value vs Cost expenditure|cost-benefit analysis]] in your work and realize that the customer/user will not need all these alleged value adds. 

The difficulty with truly understanding this is to realize when to go crazy and when to be conservative in your approach. 

The is a classic situation for engineers to be in. Excited by the opportunity to show off their own ability and prove their worth, engineers will over-engineer the piss out of something. Using jackhammers to drive a nail. Take care to architect and choose the right tool for the job.
