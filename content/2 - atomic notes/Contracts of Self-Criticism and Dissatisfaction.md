# Contracts of Self-Criticism and Dissatisfaction

![[death_of_socrates.jpg]]

We enter into self-agreed contracts with ourselves in which we give ourselves permission to act in out of the ordinary ways in exchange for progress in pursuit of some other thing. Think of the self-agreed contracts that we enter into with ourselves. How many of them are positive or negative? I would wager mostly negative. 

## Goals as Self-Contracts

Our goals are a sort of self-contract. We tell ourselves to aspire to something, and to get there we must sacrifice pieces of ourselves to that goal. Yet what are those goals but fleeting markers when accomplished. Goals that will always change and shift for what is a Human without a goal in the back of their mind? 

To accomplish these goals we destroy something within ourselves. Losing ourselves in the midst of the process. For example, I am the most competitive person you will ever meet. Outside I don't seem like it, but inside I stew with anxiety born from a fear of losing the conjured competition that I make up in my head. This propels me to do insane things that are impressive, yes, but at what cost? I can't ever slow down or the competitive advantage is gone. I can't take a rest and relax or I will never be able to catch up to those ahead of me.

## Desire as Self-Contracts

Desire can be a self-contract. One where we agree to place pieces of ourselves inside an ever elusive object. One that can't be attained so we can never get those pieces back. A chase of an ever elusive desire.

Think of when you have a crush. It is simultaneous the best and worst feeling ever. Why is that? Our emotional state becomes tethered to the whims of another person. We begin to read into every little thing they do. Such that when they do something, their actions echo back into ourselves, controlling us in a way.

## Choosing Servitude to Our Self-Contracts

Perhaps the most destructive contracts are those that combine the two. The desire to achieve goals. To become more. When we are constantly in a state of desire for a future state, we can never experience the depth of our current state. A constant and perpetual state of future facing prospects, locking us into "what ifs" rather than "what is".

We agree to the conditions of our self-contracts, only aware of the potential gain, but blissfully unaware of pieces of ourselves we are losing.
