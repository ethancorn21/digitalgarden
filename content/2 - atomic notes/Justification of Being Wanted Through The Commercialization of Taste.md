# Justification of Being Wanted Through The Commercialization of Taste

![[John_William_Waterhouse_-_Echo_and_Narcissus_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg]]

Niche this, niche that. We feel so drawn to project out aspects of the self onto social media. Aspects that we think makes us rare, rareness which we see as justification of our wanting to be prized.

## taste Through Algorithms

The commercialization of taste is already in full effect. We are pushed to see it with influencers. People who build a brand off the reputation that a cultivated style and personality can bring. Algorithms push these people because something within us (which I will get to) likes the vibe, aesthetic, vision that they bring to our feed.

Of course it's appealing to us as consumers, because there is a certain aspect of the human psyche that is drawn to that distinctiveness. So we follow these people. By consuming their content, we allow ourselves to draw inspiration from them. We begin to model certain aspects of ourselves from these people. 

Of course there is nothing wrong with this, our ancestors have been doing it since the dawn of humanity. It is inherent to being a human. We are social creatures and so naturally we look for social advantages to incorporate within ourselves. Things that make us stand out from one another. Stuff that makes us unique. But because we adopt these new aspects of ourselves to gain a social advantage, naturally of course, competition will follow. It becomes a competition to have the best taste, to be the most sophisticated, to be the most interesting. 

Algorithms whether purposefully designed or not, are taking advantage of the fact that:
1) we now have significantly larger social groups compared to our ancestors, meaning that we have to stand out among more people
2) we have an inherent desire to mimic interesting parts of people so that we can stand out

This leads us to algorithms that take advantage of a natural psychological tendency, but removes the guard rails by supernormally enlarging our social circle. These apps are designed to sell the attention and fame that seemingly comes from being unique. They sell you on being wanted. On attention. Such that, we seek to capture our own version of the attention we see others get by trying to emulate the ways in which they show they are different. We are being algorithmically sold taste as the differentiator that will make us feel wanted. 

## In the Wild

I kid you not, this is not me being a online-cel, think about the dumb shit we do before we post something online. We put on a new drippy outfit (this is aura we say), go to a place (obviously it's aesthetic), ask someone to take pictures (because we are in a special outfit in a unique place), do a stupid pose (preferably one that shows something about us that makes us unique, like our super hot face or our awesome body), pick a nonchalant caption that shows we are so casually cool and don't really care about this whole social media thing, then we spend 3 hours picking the perfect 10 second song clip that shows how good our taste in music is, and then we send it to cyberspace to await for our confirmation of acceptance.

I mean take the Pinterest app. It is literally taste slop. You're interacting with photos that are appealing to you so that you can cultivate an aesthetic. Why are we so focused on being aesthetic, or having an aesthetic. Even aura, as corny as it is, is all in pursuit of the same goal. To have a marker that we can point to, which says, "I am unique and that justifies me being wanted". 

## The Paradox of Chasing Uniqueness

If everyone is trying to differentiate themselves, who really becomes differentiated?

That is the real question, and my answer to that would of course be gotten from invoking [[Inversion Philosophy]]. The people who truly don't care about being different. Not those that feign not caring, because that is easy to see through, but those that actually could not care less about being seen as different. Am I them? No, 100% not. It's the people that are ok with not partaking in the latest trends and don't think that their non-participation makes them any different.

So I would say, as anticlimactic as it is, don't chase being different. The moment we chase being different, we cease to be so. Superficial taste is not what makes us cool.
