Bond - IOU ; ill pay you back by the set date and also give you interest
- par value (aka face value) is paid at a set future date (maturity date). 
- most times semiannual interest payments 
- bonds != ownership the same way equity does
- bond interest is tax are deductible but dividends are not
- When issuing bonds, if the company can make more money than the interest payment on those bonds, they can increase equity. Leveraged, using debt to try to make more money
Disadvantages of bonds:
- if return on equity is less than interest payments = loss of equity
- bonds require distribution of both interest payments and the par value of the bond indenture. 

Par Bonds:
- bonds issued at par value
- debit to cash
- credit to bonds payable
- semiannual interest payments
	- debit to bonds interest expense
	- credit to cash
	- every 6 months the same entry
- on maturity
	- debit to bonds payable for 100,000
	- credit to cash for 100,000
Discount Bonds
 - bought at below their par value (face value)
	 - ex: $1000 bond sold at $900
	 - associated with higher risk of issuer defaulting on payment
- issuance of discount bonds
	- debit to cash for market value of bond
	- debit to discount on bonds payable for par value - market value
	- credit to bonds payable for par value of bonds
	

contract rate = interest rate specified in bond contract (bond indenture) * par value.
- this will equal the discounted bonds price.

discount on bonds payable = bonds par value - its lower issued price. 

bond carrying value = par value - discount on bonds payable

discount on bonds payable = contra liabilities account

Amortizing Discount bonds
- $100,000 bond with 8% interest rate; lifetime of 2 years; sold at $96,400
	- 8% of 100,000 = 8000 in interest per year * 2 years = $16000
	- $16000 plus bond discount (100,000-96,400) 3,600 = 19600 interest expense.
	- Straight line method: 19600(interest expense) / (2* 2) (bond lifetime * 2 semiannual periods per year.) = 4900 payed out twice a year for 2 years.
