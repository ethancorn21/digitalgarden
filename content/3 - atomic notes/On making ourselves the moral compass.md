# On making ourselves the moral compass

![[JEAN_LOUIS_THÉODORE_GÉRICAULT_-_La_Balsa_de_la_Medusa_(Museo_del_Louvre,_1818-19).jpg]]

In the absence of a higher power, whether religious or institutional. The mind becomes the arbiter of what is moral and right. The problem is that the human moral compass can be reasoned and bargained with. 

Someone who (consciously or unconsciously) places themselves at the top of the moral control hierarchy, will and can say that they would never do a certain thing. But then, when the reward for doing a certain thing becomes greater than the shame of doing it, the moral compass begins to be reasoned with. The ego begins to shift more towards the id than the superego.

I propose that the vast majority of people, myself included, need to be bound by a moral framework lest they wander off. We are animals at the end of the day. And like animals we sometimes have to be put in our pen for the greater good. I think restoring a moral concreteness is for the greater good for the vast majority of people and society.

Moral frameworks that have punishment and reward are needed. Punishment follows wrongs. 

The only solution based counterpoint I have heard for this perspective involves empowering the reasoning of the masses so that the mechanism that involves reasoning toward the id, is reasoned toward the superego. I doubt I have to explain why this would not work. We have invested trillions of dollars to increase the logical reasoning of humans. Yet we are not a hive mind. We will cave to self-motivated desires if the rewards are great enough. 

Balancing purity spirals and moral hedonism is tricky. Solution?
