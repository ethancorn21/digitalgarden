# The Inherent Desire For Community

We inherently desire community because we are tribal animals. We evolved to be apart of a group. For hundreds of thousands of years, the homo species was now powerful, very middle of the road on the food chain. We were regularly hunted by large birds and the big cat ancestors. 

It is no wonder then that those that stuck to a community, worked together, communicated within, and sacrificed to stay within the community went on to survive and reproduce. We had to. Our group dynamics probably gave rise to our intelligence and played the lead part in our ascension to the apex predator of the entire globe.

Yet the conditions for our survival have changed drastically. Societally, we have evolved so much, and yet we have remained the same in every other aspect. How much of our society is built to console the ape desire for a tribe?

It's interesting to me how much we still conform to our desires in this way. I figure that it's because it feels too good to want to break out of the instinct. Not that it's a bad thing, community feels good. Plus, it's only through unity en masse that we can push the frontiers of the natural world.
