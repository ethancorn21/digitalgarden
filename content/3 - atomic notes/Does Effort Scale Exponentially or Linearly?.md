# Does Effort Scale Exponentially or Linearly?

I am curious about the role of compounding for total output of a system versus total input. More specifically, does energy invested in youth compound to greater results than if that energy were invested later? 

Does output per input scale according to power law or logarithmically?

Logarithmic scaling would imply that energy invested into the system has diminishing marginal utility such that their is an efficient point in life where you should stop investing energy into certain systems and begin redirecting them to where the opportunity cost is greatest. 

Power law distribution of output to input would suggest that most of the output is tied to the energy put into the system in the beginning. This is similar to what Andrew Grove says in, [[High Output Management]], that energy should be invested into systems at the beginning, while entropy is low and can be contained.

Or perhaps it is a combination of the two. At the beginning, following Pareto's principle, 80% of the results will come from 20% of the effort. Meaning that near the beginning as more effort and energy is poured into the system the critical mass builds. Once critical mass reaches super criticality, it will explode until the mass cannot sustain itself and reaches a self-regulating state. This type of hybrid state between power distribution and logarithmic scaling most closely resembles a sigmoid function.
