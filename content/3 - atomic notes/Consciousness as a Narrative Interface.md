# Consciousness as a Narrative Interface

Recent research has found that we make decisions before our brains can process those decisions. And yet, how often do we think that the self we know came to the conclusion? We begin to backfill in reasoning and logic to fit the decision we already made, not the other way around. 

In patients who have had their brain split by severing the tissue that connects the two hemispheres (corpus callosum), one brain hemisphere was given instructions to follow. The left side of your body, eyes included, are connected to the right side of your brain and vice versa. So by covering one eye and having subjects read an instruction card, researchers were able to isolate which hemisphere of the brain they were interacting with. After the subject followed the instructions, say "get up from your chair and walk around in a circle". The subject would be asked why they had done that, and the subject would almost always reply something along the lines of, "oh i just needed to walk around". 

So we can see that even though the brain did not have all the context available from both sides of the brain, one side of the brain attributed meaning to what is was doing even though it did not know the exact reasoning for why it did something. The brain jumps to conclusions to create a coherent story for itself, even if that story is fabricated.

We can abstract this out to brain function and the unconscious. If we are not conscious of our own "instructions", like non-conscious impulses and things of the like, when we move to follow those non-conscious impulses why wouldn't we assign meaning to what we are doing in the same way with the split brain experiment? 

Our decisions are mostly non-conscious, why would we not also assign meaning to our decisions? Can you imagine how many decisions you have made for no particular reason but your brain back filled in to make seem coherent.

It seems to me that consciousness is a narrative device that assigns meaning to create a coherent story so that the viewer, the self, has any clue about what we are watching. What does that say about the brain's architecture if we leap to conclusions rather than conceptualize the gaps? If our brains are adapted from our environment, what does this say about the environment in which it adapted from?
