# Nobody Hears Anybody

Every conversation is a translation, run twice. A thought with no form gets squeezed into words that were never big enough for it, and the listener runs those words through their own biases until they hold a thought of their own, “a big cloud of nothingness and everything.” Then they answer, and it happens again in reverse. Has anybody truly begun to understand anybody?

We make it worse by trying to win the exchange. In an effort to be heard, I often forget to hear. I reach for a story that is relatable to no one but me, and while I tell it, the hints the other person dropped about what they care about go by unnoticed. What they wanted was small: “I hear you and I see you, you are not crazy.”

Then we mistake the shared language for the person. A man mocked for his accent answers, “I speak English because it’s the only language you know.” Most of us talk down to the room, and so does everyone else, so the plain talk we hear is a courtesy, not a ceiling. The work is to draw people into the language they prefer, and the way in is to acknowledge their ideas.

If language is the bottleneck, someone will engineer it like any other. How fast a society trades ideas sets how fast it advances, and a language can be measured by how much it carries per word. Until something lets us pass thoughts as thoughts, the best we have is to listen as if the translation were our job.
